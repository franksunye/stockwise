# 101-06: The Endowment Effect—Why it "becomes expensive" the moment you buy it?

> *"We tend to have an irrational loyalty to the things we own."* — Richard Thaler

Imagine this: if you didn't already own that stock you're stuck with at a 20% loss, and someone asked you to buy it right now with your cold, hard cash—would you?

The answer for most retail investors is: **"Hell no, the trend is terrible."**

If you wouldn't buy it, then why haven't you sold it? This is a factory default bug in the human brain: **The Endowment Effect**.

---

## What is the Endowment Effect?

The psychological definition is simple: **Once you own something, you immediately value it more than its objective market value.**

In the famous "Coffee Mug Experiment," those given a mug demanded a selling price that was typically **twice** what those without a mug were willing to pay. Just because they "owned" it, that ordinary mug became a "sacred asset" in their eyes.

In the stock market, this psychology is deadly.

---

## How it Kills Your Account

### Emotional Premium
The moment you hit "Buy," that ticker becomes "My Asset." You subconsciously filter out negative news and amplify the positives. You haven't bought a stock; you've bought your own **expectations**.

### The Cost of Admitting Error
Selling means admitting that your initial decision was wrong. To escape this "intellectual humiliation," the brain triggers protection mechanisms:
*   "It's a value investment; I'm holding for the long term."
*   "The big money hasn't left yet; they're just shaking out the weak hands."
*   "The fundamentals haven't changed." (When in reality, you haven't even looked at the earnings report).
